Your shop shouldn't stop billing when the internet does

21 October 2026 · 5 min read

It's Saturday evening and your counter is full. Five customers are waiting, one is holding out a ₹2,000 note, and the internet goes down. With a lot of billing apps the screen now shows a spinning wheel and you're back to a notebook, guessing GST and promising to "make the bill later".

We built Universal POS so that evening never happens again. Every sale is saved on your own phone or PC first. The internet is only used later, in the background, to sync your other tills and the cloud. If the network drops, nothing at the counter changes. Your customers never find out.

Fast enough for the rush

A billing screen should never slow down the queue. We test these speeds on a budget Android phone before every release:

  • Under 50 ms from barcode scan to the item in the cart
  • 2 taps after the last scan to finish a cash sale
  • Under 150 ms to save the sale and open the cash drawer; the receipt prints in the background
  • Under 100 ms to search across 2 million products

One app on the devices you already have

Android phone or tablet, Windows or Linux PC, iPad, and an admin panel in the browser. Same screens, same data. Need a second till during Diwali? Install the app on a spare phone and pair it with your shop.

More than one till in the shop? One PC or phone becomes the shop hub and links every till over your Wi-Fi, even with no internet at all. When the internet is back, the hub sends everything to the cloud so you can check sales from home.

When your plan ends you get 30 days of grace, then read-only access. Your bills, stock and customers are never locked away or deleted.

Your data stays yours

  • No forced login, no activation server, no Google services or Firebase needed
  • Encrypted one-tap backup to a pen drive; a PC also backs itself up every day
  • Restore on a new phone or PC and your products, customers, bills and staff come back
  • Updates can be installed from a pen drive, checked before they install
  • Card terminals report approved or declined only; the app never sees card numbers
